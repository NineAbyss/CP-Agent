# Section 2 Proofs

This document contains the proofs for Section 2 ("A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control") of the paper, covering the calibration estimators after Definition 2.4, Theorem 2.5, Theorem 2.6, Propositions 2.7–2.9, Corollary 2.10, and the validity of the mechanism factorization in Eq. (9).

---

## 0. Setting and Conventions

### 0.1 Probability space and stopped events

Fix a frozen controller $\pi$. A trajectory is generated by sampling one problem from the benchmark distribution and running $\pi$ for at most $T$ refinement steps. All probabilities $\Pr_\pi(\cdot)$ are taken with respect to a fresh evaluation trajectory; calibration probabilities are taken with respect to held-out calibration trajectories.

The stopping times are

$$
\tau_F := \inf\{t : F_t = 1\}, \qquad
\tau_S := \inf\{t : S_t = 1\},
$$

and the active event before step $t$ is

$$
A_t := \{\tau_S > t-1,\; \tau_F > t-1\}.
$$

By Definition 2.1,

$$
F_t := \mathbf 1\{A_t,\,\Gamma_t = 1,\,B_t^\epsilon\}, \qquad
S_t := \mathbf 1\{A_t,\,\Gamma_t = 1,\,\neg B_t^\epsilon\},
$$

so $F_t$ and $S_t$ are mutually exclusive at every fixed step $t$ via $B_t^\epsilon$. Hence $\tau_S$ and $\tau_F$ cannot both be finite and equal (they may, however, both equal $+\infty$ when neither stop occurs by $T$, with the convention $\inf\varnothing := +\infty$). The certified event is

$$
\{\tau_S \le T,\; \tau_S < \tau_F\}.
$$

We adopt the stopped-process convention: once a clean success or false admission occurs at some step $t^\star$, $S_t$ and $F_t$ are set to $0$ at all subsequent steps $t > t^\star$ and the trajectory is inactive. As a consequence, if $\tau_S < \infty$ then $F_t = 0$ for all $t > \tau_S$, so $\tau_F = +\infty$ on $\{\tau_S < \tau_F\}$; symmetrically, $\tau_S = +\infty$ on $\{\tau_F < \tau_S\}$. We further assume $\Pr_\pi(A_1) = 1$, i.e., the trajectory begins active at step $1$.

### 0.2 Admission-gate semantics required by the mechanism factorization

Eq. (9) and Section 5 below use the containment

$$
\{F_t = 1\} \subseteq \{B_t^\epsilon = 1,\; W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\; W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1\} \quad \text{on } A_t.
$$

This is equivalent to the requirement that the observable admission gate satisfies

$$
\Gamma_t = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \;\text{and}\; W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \quad \text{on } A_t,
$$

i.e., any candidate released by $\Gamma_t$ has first passed the risk-probe and evidence stages (with the convention $W^{\mathrm{probe}} = W^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1$ when the corresponding stage is not invoked). We treat this implication as part of the manifest contract on $\pi$; if $\Gamma_{1:T}^{\mathrm{obs}}$ does not chain the upstream screens, the mechanism UCB is invalid and only the raw UCB $\bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi$ applies.

### 0.3 Held-out exchangeability

Under Assumption 2.3, calibration and evaluation trajectories are independent draws from the same benchmark distribution. Independence is required across problem trajectories at a fixed step $t$, not across refinement steps within a trajectory. For fixed $t$ and $\pi$, the active calibration rows yield conditional Bernoulli samples; a random active denominator does not invalidate the bounds, because we condition on the denominator first and then mix.

### 0.4 Zero-probability conditioning

For any conditional probability $\Pr(H \mid E)$ with $\Pr(E) = 0$, we set $\Pr(H \mid E) := 0$. This convention never inflates an upper bound; in chain-rule decompositions, the corresponding event has probability $0$ and contributes nothing.

### 0.5 Clopper–Pearson UCB / LCB

Let $Y_1, \dots, Y_n \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ and $K = \sum_i Y_i$. Write

$$
U(K, n, \eta) = \mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}(K, n, \eta), \qquad
L(K, n, \eta) = \mathrm{LCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}(K, n, \eta)
$$

for the exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson bounds, so that

$$
\Pr_p\{p \le U(K, n, \eta)\} \ge 1 - \eta, \qquad
\Pr_p\{p \ge L(K, n, \eta)\} \ge 1 - \eta.
$$

If the denominator $N$ is random with $K \mid N = n \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n, p)$, then

$$
\Pr\{p > U(K, N, \eta)\}
= \sum_n \Pr(N = n)\,\Pr\{p > U(K, n, \eta) \mid N = n\}
\le \eta,
$$

and the analogous bound holds for the LCB. We use the conservative conventions

$$
\mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}(0, 0, \eta) = 1, \qquad
\mathrm{LCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}(0, 0, \eta) = 0.
$$

### 0.6 Pre-declaration of score-defining objects

The proof of Theorem 2.5 requires that the score function $r_t(Z_t) = \rho_t \exp(-I_t)$, the finite threshold grid $\Theta_t$ (with $1 \le |\Theta_t| < \infty$), and the gate rule $\mathbf 1\{r_t \le \theta\}$ be determined by $\pi$ alone, using only the observable trajectory state. Concretely:

1. The measurable map $\phi_\pi : X_t \mapsto Z_t = (\rho_t, I_t, \underline h_t, t)$ depends only on observable mechanism outputs (probe survival, evidence-gate survival, retrieval slots, step index), not on hidden labels $B_t^\epsilon$.
2. The finite grid $\Theta_t$ is determined by $\pi$ with $1 \le |\Theta_t| < \infty$.
3. The gate rule $\mathbf 1\{r_t \le \theta\}$ is determined by $\pi$.

If $\phi_\pi$, $\Theta_t$, the aggregation rule $\gamma_{\mathrm{evid}}$, the memory snapshot $M^\star$, or any calibrated scalar in $r_t$ (such as $\bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}$ or $\bar b_t^\pi$) is tuned on $D_{\mathrm{cal}}$, every candidate value must be enumerated in the finite, pre-declared manifest class $\Pi_\alpha$ (with $1 \le |\Pi_\alpha| < \infty$) in advance, so that the union bound of Theorem 2.6 absorbs both the score selection and the gate threshold selection. Throughout Theorem 2.5 and Theorem 2.6 we treat this pre-declaration as a standing hypothesis.

## 1. Validity of the Calibration Estimators in Eq. (7)

For fixed $\pi$ and step $t$, let $n_t^\pi$ be the number of active calibration rows, $f_t^\pi$ the count of false admissions, and $s_t^\pi$ the count of clean successes. Define

$$
\bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi := \mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(f_t^\pi, n_t^\pi, \tfrac{\delta}{2T}\right),
\qquad
\underline h_t^\pi := \mathrm{LCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(s_t^\pi, n_t^\pi, \tfrac{\delta}{2T}\right).
\tag{7}
$$

The deployment hazards are $q_t^\pi := \Pr_\pi(F_t = 1 \mid A_t)$ and $h_t^\pi := \Pr_\pi(S_t = 1 \mid A_t)$.

**Proof.** Fix $t$ and $\pi$. Let $A_{i,t}$ be the active indicator for calibration trajectory $i$. On $\{A_{i,t} = 1\}$, $F_{i,t}$ is Bernoulli with parameter $q_t^\pi$. By Assumption 2.3, different trajectories are independent at fixed $t$, so the active rows yield i.i.d. $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(q_t^\pi)$ samples; the random denominator $n_t^\pi$ is handled by Section 0.5. The Clopper–Pearson UCB gives

$$
\Pr\{q_t^\pi \le \bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi\} \ge 1 - \tfrac{\delta}{2T}.
$$

The same argument applied to $S_{i,t}$ gives $\Pr\{h_t^\pi \ge \underline h_t^\pi\} \ge 1 - \tfrac{\delta}{2T}$. A union bound over the $2T$ one-sided events $t = 1, \dots, T$ yields

$$
\Pr\!\left\{\forall t \le T:\;
q_t^\pi \le \bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi
\;\text{and}\;
h_t^\pi \ge \underline h_t^\pi\right\} \ge 1 - \delta. \qquad\square
$$

The argument requires only across-trajectory independence at fixed $t$, not within-trajectory step independence.

## 2. Theorem 2.5: Calibrated Admission Gate

**Statement.** Assume the pre-declaration of Section 0.6: $\phi_\pi$, the score function $r_t(Z_t) = \rho_t \exp(-I_t)$, the finite non-empty grid $\Theta_t$ (with $1 \le |\Theta_t| < \infty$), and the gate rule are fixed by $\pi$ before label counting; any calibrated scalar entering $r_t$ is enumerated in the finite, non-empty manifest $\Pi_\alpha$ (with $1 \le |\Pi_\alpha| < \infty$). On held-out calibration traces, let $n_t(\theta)$ be the number of active candidate admissions with $r_t(Z_t) \le \theta$ and $f_t(\theta)$ the count of such admissions rejected by hidden evaluation. Define

$$
\bar R_t(\theta) := \mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(f_t(\theta),\, n_t(\theta),\, \tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{T |\Theta_t|}\right)
$$

and select

$$
\hat\theta_{t,\alpha} = \max\{\theta \in \Theta_t : \bar R_t(\theta) \le \alpha_t\},
\tag{8}
$$

with $\hat\theta_{t,\alpha} := -\infty$ and $\Gamma_t^\alpha \equiv 0$ when the set is empty. Then, with calibration probability at least $1 - \delta_{\mathrm{gate}}$, the gate $\Gamma_t^\alpha = \mathbf 1\{r_t(Z_t) \le \hat\theta_{t,\alpha}\}$ satisfies

$$
\Pr(F_t = 1 \mid A_t,\, \Gamma_t^\alpha = 1) \le \alpha_t
$$

simultaneously for all $t \le T$ and all data-dependent $\hat\theta_{t,\alpha} \in \Theta_t$.

**Proof.** Section 0.6 asserts that $\phi_\pi$, $\Theta_t$, and the gate rule are pre-declared (or absorbed into $\Pi_\alpha$ by Theorem 2.6). Fix $t$ and $\theta \in \Theta_t$, and define the deployment conditional rejection rate

$$
p_{t,\theta} := \Pr_\pi(F_t = 1 \mid A_t,\, r_t(Z_t) \le \theta).
$$

The conditioning event $\{A_t,\, r_t(Z_t) \le \theta\}$ is determined by observables. By Assumption 2.3, the calibration rows satisfying this event yield i.i.d. $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_{t,\theta})$ samples for hidden rejection, with a random denominator $n_t(\theta)$ handled by Section 0.5. The Clopper–Pearson UCB gives

$$
\Pr\{p_{t,\theta} \le \bar R_t(\theta)\} \ge 1 - \tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{T |\Theta_t|}.
$$

Union-bounding over $t \le T$ and $\theta \in \Theta_t$ defines the good event

$$
G_{\mathrm{gate}} := \{\forall t \le T,\, \forall \theta \in \Theta_t:\; p_{t,\theta} \le \bar R_t(\theta)\},
\quad
\Pr(G_{\mathrm{gate}}) \ge 1 - \delta_{\mathrm{gate}}.
$$

On $G_{\mathrm{gate}}$, consider the data-dependent choice $\hat\theta_{t,\alpha}$.

*Case 1: the selection set is non-empty.* Then $\hat\theta_{t,\alpha} \in \Theta_t$ and $\bar R_t(\hat\theta_{t,\alpha}) \le \alpha_t$. Since $G_{\mathrm{gate}}$ holds at every grid point,

$$
p_{t,\hat\theta_{t,\alpha}} \le \bar R_t(\hat\theta_{t,\alpha}) \le \alpha_t,
$$

and $\Gamma_t^\alpha = 1$ is equivalent to $r_t(Z_t) \le \hat\theta_{t,\alpha}$, so

$$
\Pr(F_t = 1 \mid A_t,\, \Gamma_t^\alpha = 1) = p_{t,\hat\theta_{t,\alpha}} \le \alpha_t.
$$

*Case 2: the selection set is empty.* Then $\Gamma_t^\alpha \equiv 0$, and $\Pr(A_t,\, \Gamma_t^\alpha = 1) = 0$; by Section 0.4 the conditional probability is $0 \le \alpha_t$. $\qquad\square$

The post-selection validity follows from establishing the UCB simultaneously over the finite grid before any data-dependent choice is made.

## 3. Proposition 2.7: RiskProbe Bad-and-Survive UCB

**Statement.** Let $W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} \in \{0, 1\}$ be the probe-survival indicator at step $t$ (set to $1$ when the probe is not invoked). On held-out calibration traces, let $n_t^\pi$ be the number of active rows at step $t$ under $\pi$, and let $f_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}$ count those rows where both $B_t^\epsilon = 1$ and $W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1$. Define

$$
\bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi} := \mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(f_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi},\, n_t^\pi,\, \tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}}{T}\right).
$$

Then, with calibration probability at least $1 - \delta_{\mathrm{probe}}$, simultaneously for $t \le T$,

$$
\bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi} \ge \Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \mid A_t),
$$

and

$$
\Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \mid A_t)
= \sigma_t^{\mathrm{bad},\pi}\,\rho_t^\pi,
$$

where $\sigma_t^{\mathrm{bad},\pi} := \Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon,\, A_t)$ and $\rho_t^\pi := \Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon \mid A_t)$.

**Proof.** Fix $t$. For calibration trajectory $i$, let

$$
Y_{i,t}^{\mathrm{probe}} := \mathbf 1\{A_{i,t} = 1,\, B_{i,t}^\epsilon = 1,\, W_{i,t}^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1\}
$$

and consider it only on active rows. On $\{A_{i,t} = 1\}$,

$$
\mathbf 1\{B_{i,t}^\epsilon = 1,\, W_{i,t}^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1\}
$$

is Bernoulli with parameter $p_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi} := \Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \mid A_t)$. By Assumption 2.3, the active rows yield i.i.d. samples; Section 0.5 handles the random denominator. The Clopper–Pearson UCB gives

$$
\Pr\{p_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi} \le \bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\} \ge 1 - \tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}}{T},
$$

and a union bound over $t = 1, \dots, T$ yields the simultaneous statement with confidence $1 - \delta_{\mathrm{probe}}$.

The factorization follows from the chain rule of conditional probability (no independence is required):

$$
\Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \mid A_t)
= \Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon,\, A_t)
\,\Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon \mid A_t)
= \sigma_t^{\mathrm{bad},\pi}\,\rho_t^\pi. \qquad\square
$$

If $\sigma_t^{\mathrm{bad},\pi} < 1$, the probe contracts the bad-and-survive mass strictly below the bad prior; if additionally $\sigma_t^{\mathrm{bad},\pi} < \sigma_t^{\mathrm{good},\pi}$, the probe also shrinks the bad-versus-good odds.

## 4. Proposition 2.8: Candidate-Level Evidence from EvidenceAcquire

**Statement.** Let $W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} \in \{0, 1\}$ denote the program-level evidence-gate survival at step $t$ (set to $1$ when the gate is not invoked). On calibration rows that are simultaneously active, bad, and probe-surviving, let $\bar b_t^\pi(m)$ be the one-sided Clopper–Pearson UCB on

$$
\Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, A_t)
$$

at level $\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}/T$. Set $\Delta I_t(m) := -\log \bar b_t^\pi(m)$. Then, with calibration probability at least $1 - \delta_{\mathrm{evid}}$, simultaneously for $t \le T$,

$$
\Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, A_t)
\le \exp[-\Delta I_t(m)].
$$

**Proof.** Fix $t$ and restrict the calibration set to rows with $A_t = 1$, $B_t^\epsilon = 1$, and $W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1$. Let $n_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}$ be the number of such rows and $k_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}$ the number with $W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1$. Conditional on this restriction, $W_t^{\mathrm{evid}}$ is Bernoulli with parameter

$$
b_t^\pi(m) := \Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, A_t).
$$

By Assumption 2.3 and Section 0.5, the Clopper–Pearson UCB gives

$$
\Pr\{b_t^\pi(m) \le \bar b_t^\pi(m)\} \ge 1 - \tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}}{T},
$$

and a union bound over $t = 1, \dots, T$ produces the good event

$$
G_{\mathrm{evid}} := \{\forall t \le T:\; b_t^\pi(m) \le \bar b_t^\pi(m)\}, \quad \Pr(G_{\mathrm{evid}}) \ge 1 - \delta_{\mathrm{evid}}.
$$

On $G_{\mathrm{evid}}$, $\exp[-\Delta I_t(m)] = \bar b_t^\pi(m)$, so

$$
\Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, A_t)
= b_t^\pi(m) \le \bar b_t^\pi(m) = \exp[-\Delta I_t(m)].
$$

If the conditioning subset is empty, $\bar b_t^\pi(m) = 1$ by Section 0.5 and the bound is trivial. $\qquad\square$

### 4.1 Suite-level interpretation

The bound treats the all-pass event over the consensus-filtered suite as a single Bernoulli channel,

$$
W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \iff \text{a bad-and-probe-surviving candidate passes the entire suite}.
$$

We calibrate the suite-level rate $b_t^\pi(m)$ directly; the $m$ generated tests need not be independent, and $m$ is not treated as a sample size.

### 4.2 Channel-level accumulation across refinement steps

To match the cumulative form $\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi} := \sum_{s \le t} \Delta I_s(m_s)$ used in Definition 2.1, Proposition 2.8, and Eq. (9) of the main text, we now generalize Section 4 to allow evidence channels to be indexed jointly by refinement step $s \le t$ and any within-step parallel sub-channel $j \le J_s$. Write the channel index as $\ell = (s, j)$ and order $\mathcal L_t := \{(s,j) : 1 \le s \le t,\ 1 \le j \le J_s\}$ lexicographically. The manifest contract here is that the step-$t$ evidence gate is the accumulated suite over all such channels: the step-$t$ candidate is screened against every channel in $\mathcal L_t$, and $W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1$ iff every channel survives. (For an implementation that re-uses past channels' inputs/labels but refreshes them on the new candidate, this is the natural sequential composition; the manifest must declare which channels feed into the step-$t$ gate.)

For each $\ell = (s, j) \in \mathcal L_t$, let $E_\ell$ denote the indicator that the step-$t$ candidate passes channel $\ell$, and require sequential conditional calibration

$$
\Pr_\pi(E_\ell = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, A_t,\, E_{\ell'} = 1 \text{ for all } \ell' < \ell) \le b_\ell,
$$

where each $b_\ell$ is a Clopper–Pearson UCB at level $\delta_{\mathrm{evid}} / (T \cdot L_{\max} \cdot |\Pi_\alpha|)$ on its conditional subset, with $L_{\max} := \max_t |\mathcal L_t|$. Then by the chain rule of conditional probability,

$$
\Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, A_t)
= \Pr_\pi\!\Bigl(\bigcap_{\ell \in \mathcal L_t}\{E_\ell = 1\}\,\Big|\,\cdots\Bigr)
\le \prod_{\ell \in \mathcal L_t} b_\ell.
$$

Defining the per-channel increment $\Delta I_\ell := -\log b_\ell$, the cumulative evidence

$$
\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}
:= \sum_{\ell \in \mathcal L_t} \Delta I_\ell
= \sum_{s \le t} \underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{J_s}\!\Delta I_{(s,j)}}_{=:\,\Delta I_s(m_s)}
= \sum_{s \le t} \Delta I_s(m_s)
$$

satisfies, simultaneously for $t \le T$,

$$
\Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, A_t)
\le \exp[-\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}].
$$

This is the cumulative form used by Eq. (9) and reduces to the single-step bound of Proposition 2.8 when only the step-$t$ channels are calibrated and earlier $\Delta I_s$ ($s < t$) are set to $0$ by the conservative convention of Section 0.5 (an uncalibrated channel does not contribute evidence).

**Required manifest discipline.** For the accumulation to be valid, the manifest must declare (i) which channels feed into the step-$t$ admission gate ($\mathcal L_t$), (ii) a fixed ordering of those channels, and (iii) the calibration rule for each $b_\ell$ on its sequential conditional subset. Channels that are not refreshed on the current candidate (e.g., a past brute-force counterexample that has already been incorporated into the input distribution) provide no new evidence and must be assigned $\Delta I_\ell = 0$; otherwise the chain-rule decomposition double-counts and the resulting $\bar q_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^\pi$ may fail to be a valid UCB.

**CP-Agent instantiation.** CP-Agent uses a single Test-Augmentation channel per refinement step ($J_s \equiv 1$), so $|\mathcal L_t| = t$ and $\Delta I_s(m_s)$ is the step-$s$ TA increment. In the LCB-Pro Pass@1 setting ($T = 1$), the sum reduces to $\mathcal I_1^{\mathrm{evid},\pi} = \Delta I_1(m)$ and matches the single-step Proposition 2.8 bound; in the Refine@$T$ setting with $T > 1$, only the steps at which TA was actually invoked contribute non-zero $\Delta I_s$, and uninvoked steps contribute $0$.

## 5. Validity of the Mechanism Factorization in Eq. (9)

Eq. (9) defines

$$
\bar q_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^\pi = \bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\,\exp[-\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}],
\qquad
\bar q_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^\pi = \min\{\bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi,\, \bar q_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^\pi\}.
\tag{9}
$$

We show that on the good events of Propositions 2.7 and 2.8, $\bar q_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^\pi$ is a valid UCB on $q_t^\pi := \Pr_\pi(F_t = 1 \mid A_t)$.

**Proof.** Under the gate-semantics requirement of Section 0.2, on $A_t$,

$$
\{F_t = 1\} \subseteq \{B_t^\epsilon = 1,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1\}.
$$

The chain rule of conditional probability gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_t^\pi
&\le \Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon = 1,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \mid A_t) \\
&= \Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon = 1,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \mid A_t) \\
&\quad \times \Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon = 1,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, A_t).
\end{aligned}
$$

On the good event of Proposition 2.7,

$$
\Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon = 1,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \mid A_t) \le \bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}.
$$

On the good event of Proposition 2.8 (or Section 4.2 when multiple channels are present),

$$
\Pr_\pi(W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1 \mid B_t^\epsilon = 1,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1,\, A_t)
\le \exp[-\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}],
$$

where $\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi} = \sum_{s \le t} \Delta I_s(m_s)$ accumulates over all channels in $\mathcal L_t$ (refinement-step index $s$ and any within-step sub-channels), in the sense of Section 4.2 above. Combining,

$$
q_t^\pi \le \bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi} \exp[-\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}] = \bar q_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^\pi.
$$

Section 1 gives $q_t^\pi \le \bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi$ on its own good event. On the joint good event,

$$
q_t^\pi \le \min\{\bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi,\, \bar q_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^\pi\} = \bar q_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^\pi. \qquad\square
$$

If a mechanism factor is not separately calibrated, we set it to $1$; this can only relax $\bar q_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^\pi$, so Eq. (9) falls back to $\bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi$ without losing validity.

## 6. Proposition 2.9: ContextAcquire as a Calibrated Hazard Envelope

**Statement.** Under the split $D_{\mathrm{hist}}^{\mathrm{mem}} \to D_{\mathrm{cal}} \to D_{\mathrm{test}}$ and a frozen memory snapshot $M^\star$, ContextAcquire$(k)$ assigns $\underline h_{t+} \leftarrow \underline h_t^\pi(M^\star)$, where

$$
\underline h_t^\pi(M^\star) = \mathrm{LCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(s_t^{M^\star,\pi},\, n_t^{M^\star,\pi},\, \eta_h\right)
$$

is a Clopper–Pearson LCB estimated on calibration traces using $M^\star$. The increment $\Delta h_t(k, Z_t) := \underline h_t^\pi(M^\star) - \underline h_t^{\pi,\varnothing}$ is the snapshot-on/off LCB gap, used only as a deployment diagnostic; the certificate uses $\underline h_t^\pi(M^\star)$ directly.

**Proof.** Fix $\pi$, $t$, and $M^\star$. The split ensures that $M^\star$ is constructed from $D_{\mathrm{hist}}^{\mathrm{mem}}$ and used read-only on $D_{\mathrm{cal}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{test}}$, so the evaluation rows do not leak into memory construction. If $M^\star$ or any other manifest entry is selected from a finite set on $D_{\mathrm{cal}}$, all candidates are absorbed into the union bound of Theorem 2.6; the present statement is for a fixed $M^\star$.

Let $n_t^{M^\star,\pi}$ be the number of active calibration rows using $M^\star$ and $s_t^{M^\star,\pi}$ the count of clean successes. The deployment hazard is

$$
h_t^\pi(M^\star) := \Pr_\pi(S_t = 1 \mid A_t,\, M^\star).
$$

By Assumption 2.3 and Section 0.5, on $\{A_t = 1\}$ the indicator $S_t$ is i.i.d. Bernoulli with parameter $h_t^\pi(M^\star)$, and the Clopper–Pearson LCB satisfies

$$
\Pr\{h_t^\pi(M^\star) \ge \underline h_t^\pi(M^\star)\} \ge 1 - \eta_h.
$$

Union-bounding over steps and any manifest-level memory/controller choices yields the simultaneous LCB. Assigning $\underline h_{t+} \leftarrow \underline h_t^\pi(M^\star)$ gives $h_t^+ \ge \underline h_t^\pi(M^\star)$. The certificate (Corollary 2.10, Theorem 2.6) uses this LCB directly, not the gap $\Delta h_t(k, Z_t)$. $\qquad\square$

## 7. Corollary 2.10: Stopped Clean-Before-False Certificate

**Statement.** Fix a frozen controller $\pi$. Assume the stopped-process convention of Section 0.1 and the initial-activity condition $\Pr_\pi(A_1) = 1$. On a calibration good event giving simultaneous active-step bounds $q_t^\pi \le \bar q_t^\pi$ and $h_t^\pi \ge \underline h_t^\pi$ for all $t \le T$, a fresh evaluation trajectory satisfies

$$
\Pr_\pi(\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F)
\ge C_T(\pi)
:= \left[
\prod_{t=1}^T (1 - \bar q_t^\pi)
- \prod_{t=1}^T (1 - \underline h_t^\pi)
\right]_+ .
\tag{13}
$$

**Proof.** The argument uses only the chain rule on stopped events; no across-step independence is required.

*Step 1 (no-false survival).* The event $\{F_t = 1\}$ implies the trajectory is active at $t$, hence $\{F_t = 1\} \subseteq A_t \subseteq \{\tau_F > t-1\}$. For events $E \subseteq B \subseteq C$ with $\Pr(B) > 0$,

$$
\Pr(E \mid C) = \frac{\Pr(E)}{\Pr(C)} \le \frac{\Pr(E)}{\Pr(B)} = \Pr(E \mid B),
$$

so

$$
\Pr_\pi(F_t = 1 \mid \tau_F > t-1)
\le \Pr_\pi(F_t = 1 \mid A_t) = q_t^\pi \le \bar q_t^\pi.
$$

If $\Pr(A_t) = 0$, Section 0.4 sets the conditional to $0$ and the inequality remains valid. Iterating the chain rule on $\{\tau_F > t\} = \{\tau_F > t-1\} \cap \{F_t = 0\}$,

$$
\Pr_\pi(\tau_F > T)
= \prod_{t=1}^T \Pr_\pi(F_t = 0 \mid \tau_F > t-1)
\ge \prod_{t=1}^T (1 - \bar q_t^\pi).
\tag{14}
$$

*Step 2 (still-active upper bound).* Since $A_{t+1} = A_t \cap \{S_t = 0,\, F_t = 0\} \subseteq A_t \cap \{S_t = 0\}$,

$$
\Pr_\pi(A_{t+1} \mid A_t) \le \Pr_\pi(S_t = 0 \mid A_t) = 1 - h_t^\pi \le 1 - \underline h_t^\pi.
$$

Iterating, and using $\Pr_\pi(A_1) = 1$ from the Section 0.1 initial-activity assumption together with the inclusion $A_{T+1} \subseteq A_T \subseteq \cdots \subseteq A_1$,

$$
\Pr_\pi(A_{T+1}) = \prod_{t=1}^T \Pr_\pi(A_{t+1} \mid A_t)
\le \prod_{t=1}^T (1 - \underline h_t^\pi).
\tag{15}
$$

*Step 3 (decomposition).* We claim the disjoint identity

$$
\{\tau_F > T\} = \{\tau_S \le T,\; \tau_S < \tau_F\} \;\dot\cup\; A_{T+1}.
$$

To verify it, fix a trajectory in $\{\tau_F > T\}$ and split on whether $\tau_S \le T$ or $\tau_S > T$:

(i) If $\tau_S \le T$ then $\tau_S \le T < \tau_F$, so the trajectory lies in $\{\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F\}$.

(ii) If $\tau_S > T$ then $\tau_S > T$ and $\tau_F > T$, hence the trajectory lies in $A_{T+1} = \{\tau_S > T,\, \tau_F > T\}$.

For the reverse containment, note that the two right-hand events are disjoint: in $A_{T+1}$ we have $\tau_S > T$, contradicting $\tau_S \le T$. To see that every trajectory in $\{\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F\}$ also lies in $\{\tau_F > T\}$, we invoke the stopped-process convention of Section 0.1: on $\{\tau_S < \tau_F\}$, $\tau_S$ is the first stop, so $F_t = 0$ at every active step $t < \tau_S$ (by minimality of $\tau_S$) and $F_t = 0$ at every step $t > \tau_S$ (by the stopped-process zeroing); the within-step exclusivity of $F_{\tau_S}$ and $S_{\tau_S}$ via $B_t^\epsilon$ (Section 0.1) gives $F_{\tau_S} = 0$. Hence $\tau_F = \inf\{t : F_t = 1\} = +\infty > T$, and the trajectory is in $\{\tau_F > T\}$. Without this convention the identity fails (e.g., $\tau_S = 2,\,\tau_F = 3,\,T = 5$ would satisfy $\tau_S \le T,\,\tau_S < \tau_F$ but $\tau_F \le T$).

Therefore

$$
\Pr_\pi(\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F) = \Pr_\pi(\tau_F > T) - \Pr_\pi(A_{T+1}).
$$

Combining (14) and (15) and taking the positive part,

$$
\Pr_\pi(\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F)
\ge \left[\prod_{t=1}^T (1 - \bar q_t^\pi) - \prod_{t=1}^T (1 - \underline h_t^\pi)\right]_+ . \qquad\square
$$

*Specialization to $T = 1$.* $C_1(\pi) = [\underline h_1^\pi - \bar q_1^\pi]_+$, recovering the Pass@1 form.

## 8. Theorem 2.6: Simultaneously Valid Finite-Manifest Calibration

**Statement.** Let $\Pi_\alpha$ be a finite, pre-declared, non-empty class of frozen controllers with $1 \le |\Pi_\alpha| < \infty$, declared before $D_{\mathrm{cal}}$ is opened. Each $\pi \in \Pi_\alpha$ specifies the stop/refine rule, risk-probe invocation policy, evidence intensity $m$, context intensity $k$, prompts, routing and deduplication rules, decoding parameters, retrievable memory snapshot $M^\star$, write mode $\omega_{\mathrm{write}}$, and (when a threshold gate is used) a finite non-empty grid $\Theta_t$ with $1 \le |\Theta_t| < \infty$ per step. With probability at least $1 - \delta$ over calibration, the UCB/LCB quantities entering

$$
C_T^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi)
:= \left[\prod_{t=1}^T (1 - \bar q_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^\pi) - \prod_{t=1}^T (1 - \underline h_t^\pi(M))\right]_+
\tag{11}
$$

are simultaneously valid for every $\pi \in \Pi_\alpha$, so the data-dependent selection

$$
\pi^\star \in \arg\max_{\pi \in \Pi_\alpha} C_T^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi)
\tag{12}
$$

inherits a post-selection-valid lower bound $C_T^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi^\star)$ on $\Pr_{\pi^\star}(\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F)$.

### 8.1 Confidence budget

Choose non-negative $\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}, \delta_{\mathrm{gate}}, \delta_{\mathrm{probe}}, \delta_{\mathrm{evid}}$ with

$$
\delta_{\mathrm{raw}} + \delta_{\mathrm{gate}} + \delta_{\mathrm{probe}} + \delta_{\mathrm{evid}} \le \delta. \tag{G}
$$

For each $\pi \in \Pi_\alpha$, allocate per-row Clopper–Pearson confidences as

$$
\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}}{2T |\Pi_\alpha|}, \quad
\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{T |\Theta_t| |\Pi_\alpha|}, \quad
\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}}{T |\Pi_\alpha|}, \quad
\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}}{T |\Pi_\alpha| \cdot L_{\max}}
$$

for the raw, gate, probe, and evidence bounds, respectively, with $L_{\max} := \max_t |\mathcal L_t|$ the maximum number of evidence channels (refinement-step + within-step) feeding any single step's admission gate. For CP-Agent's single-channel-per-step instantiation, $|\mathcal L_t| = t$ and hence $L_{\max} = T$. Substituting $\delta = 0.10$, $\delta_\bullet = 0.025$, $|\Pi_\alpha| = 5$, $T = 5$, $|\Theta_t| = 1$ gives the per-row raw confidence $0.025 / (2 \cdot 5 \cdot 5) = 5 \times 10^{-4}$ used in the main text, and the per-channel evidence confidence $\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}/(T \cdot |\Pi_\alpha| \cdot L_{\max}) = 0.025 / (5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5) = 2 \times 10^{-4}$ on ICPC and $5 \times 10^{-3}$ on LCB-Pro ($T = L_{\max} = 1$).

### 8.2 Proof

For each $\pi \in \Pi_\alpha$ define the good events

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi) &: \forall t,\; q_t^\pi \le \bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi \text{ and } h_t^\pi(M) \ge \underline h_t^\pi(M), \\
G_{\mathrm{gate}}(\pi) &: \forall t,\,\forall \theta \in \Theta_t,\; p_{t,\theta}^\pi \le \bar R_t^\pi(\theta), \\
G_{\mathrm{probe}}(\pi) &: \forall t,\; \Pr_\pi(B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1 \mid A_t) \le \bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}, \\
G_{\mathrm{evid}}(\pi) &: \forall t,\,\forall \ell \in \mathcal L_t^\pi,\;
\Pr_\pi(E_\ell = 1 \mid \cdots) \le b_\ell^\pi.
\end{aligned}
$$

The per-row allocations of Section 8.1 give

$$
\Pr(G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi)^c) \le \tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}}{|\Pi_\alpha|}, \quad
\Pr(G_{\mathrm{gate}}(\pi)^c) \le \tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{|\Pi_\alpha|}, \quad
\Pr(G_{\mathrm{probe}}(\pi)^c) \le \tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}}{|\Pi_\alpha|}, \quad
\Pr(G_{\mathrm{evid}}(\pi)^c) \le \tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}}{|\Pi_\alpha|}.
$$

Let

$$
G := \bigcap_{\pi \in \Pi_\alpha}\!\bigl(G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi) \cap G_{\mathrm{gate}}(\pi) \cap G_{\mathrm{probe}}(\pi) \cap G_{\mathrm{evid}}(\pi)\bigr).
$$

A union bound over $\Pi_\alpha$, steps, threshold grids, mechanism factors, and channels gives

$$
\Pr(G) \ge 1 - (\delta_{\mathrm{raw}} + \delta_{\mathrm{gate}} + \delta_{\mathrm{probe}} + \delta_{\mathrm{evid}}) \ge 1 - \delta.
$$

On $G$, for any $\pi \in \Pi_\alpha$ and any $t \le T$:

1. $q_t^\pi \le \bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi$ from $G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi)$.
2. $q_t^\pi \le \bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe},\pi} \exp[-\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}] = \bar q_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^\pi$ from Section 5 using $G_{\mathrm{probe}}(\pi) \cap G_{\mathrm{evid}}(\pi)$.
3. Therefore $q_t^\pi \le \bar q_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^\pi := \min\{\bar q_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^\pi,\, \bar q_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^\pi\}$.
4. $h_t^\pi(M) \ge \underline h_t^\pi(M)$ from $G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi)$.

Corollary 2.10 then gives $\Pr_\pi(\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F) \ge C_T^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi)$ for every $\pi \in \Pi_\alpha$. Since $G$ holds simultaneously for all $\pi \in \Pi_\alpha$, any data-dependent $\pi^\star$ in this finite class also satisfies

$$
\Pr_{\pi^\star}(\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F) \ge C_T^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi^\star).
$$

If $\pi^\star$'s manifest includes a score-threshold admission rule, $G_{\mathrm{gate}}(\pi^\star)$ provides the Theorem 2.5 guarantee at the corresponding threshold. $\qquad\square$

## 9. Monotonicity of the Certificate

In the unclipped regime, define

$$
C_T = \prod_{s=1}^T (1 - \bar q_s) - \prod_{s=1}^T (1 - \underline h_s),
\qquad
\bar q_t = \bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe}} \exp(-\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid}}).
$$

We have

$$
\frac{\partial C_T}{\partial \bar q_t} = -\prod_{s \ne t} (1 - \bar q_s) \le 0,
$$

so any reduction in $\bar q_t$ does not decrease $C_T$. Since

$$
\frac{\partial \bar q_t}{\partial \bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe}}} = \exp(-\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid}}) \ge 0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial \bar q_t}{\partial \mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid}}} = -\bar q_t \le 0,
$$

it follows that

$$
\frac{\partial C_T}{\partial \bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe}}} \le 0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial C_T}{\partial \mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid}}} \ge 0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial C_T}{\partial \underline h_t} = \prod_{s \ne t} (1 - \underline h_s) \ge 0.
$$

RiskProbe contracts $\bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe}}$, EvidenceAcquire increases $\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid}}$, and ContextAcquire raises $\underline h_t$, each weakly increasing the certificate. The same conclusions hold in the clipped regime by subgradient monotonicity of $[\cdot]_+$.

## 10. Summary of Structural Conditions

1. Across-trajectory independence at fixed $t$ is required; within-trajectory step independence is not (Section 0.3).
2. The suite-level Bernoulli treatment does not require independence across the $m$ generated tests (Section 4.1); test-level independence is needed only for sequential channel accumulation (Section 4.2).
3. The Hypothesis Prober contributes only to $\bar\rho_t^{\mathrm{probe}}$; Solution Validator and Test Augmentation contribute only to $\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid}}$ (Sections 3, 4, 5).
4. Finite grids and finite manifests give post-selection validity (Theorems 2.5, 2.6).
5. Empty cells use the conservative conventions $\mathrm{UCB}(0, 0, \eta) = 1$ and $\mathrm{LCB}(0, 0, \eta) = 0$ (Section 0.5).
6. Memory must be frozen, or all candidate snapshots, routes, and retrieval budgets must be absorbed into $\Pi_\alpha$ (Section 6).
7. Corollary 2.10 lower-bounds $\Pr(\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F)$, which is strictly stricter than aggregate Refine@$T$ or Pass@1.
8. $F_t$ and $S_t$ are mutually exclusive by construction; $\tau_S = \tau_F$ does not occur (Section 0.1).
9. The mechanism containment requires $\Gamma_t = 1 \Rightarrow W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = W_t^{\mathrm{evid}} = 1$ on $A_t$; without it, only the raw UCB applies (Section 0.2).
10. The score map $\phi_\pi$, threshold grid $\Theta_t$, aggregation rule $\gamma_{\mathrm{evid}}$, memory snapshot $M^\star$, and any calibrated scalar in $r_t$ must be pre-declared or absorbed into $\Pi_\alpha$ before label counting (Section 0.6).
11. Evidence accumulates additively across refinement steps $s \le t$ (and any within-step parallel sub-channels), giving the cumulative form $\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi} = \sum_{s \le t} \Delta I_s(m_s)$ used in Definition 2.1, Proposition 2.8, and Eq. (9); each $\Delta I_s$ must be calibrated under sequential conditional survival of all prior channels on the step-$t$ candidate (Section 4.2). Uninvoked or non-refreshed channels contribute $\Delta I_s = 0$ by Section 0.5; CP-Agent's single-channel-per-step instantiation reduces to $\mathcal I_t^{\mathrm{evid},\pi} = \sum_{s \le t} \Delta I_s(m_s)$, with $\Delta I_1(m) = \mathcal I_1^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}$ in the $T=1$ Pass@1 setting.
12. The confidence budget satisfies $\delta_{\mathrm{raw}} + \delta_{\mathrm{gate}} + \delta_{\mathrm{probe}} + \delta_{\mathrm{evid}} \le \delta$ (Section 8.1).

## 11. Compact Proof Skeleton

1. *Lemma (Clopper–Pearson with random active denominator).* Conditioning on the active-row count, the CP UCB/LCB is valid; a union bound covers steps, grids, and manifests.
2. *Theorem 2.5.* For each $(t, \theta)$, calibrate $p_{t,\theta} = \Pr(F_t = 1 \mid A_t,\, r_t \le \theta)$; the finite-grid simultaneous UCB yields a post-selection-valid threshold.
3. *Proposition 2.7.* On active rows, $\mathbf 1\{B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}} = 1\}$ is Bernoulli; the chain rule gives the factorization $\sigma_t^{\mathrm{bad}} \rho_t$.
4. *Proposition 2.8.* On bad-and-probe-surviving rows, calibrate the suite-level Bernoulli survival; $\Delta I = -\log \bar b$ requires no independence across the $m$ tests; channel-level accumulation requires sequential conditional calibration.
5. *Eq. (9).* The chain rule on $\{F_t = 1\} \subseteq \{B_t^\epsilon,\, W_t^{\mathrm{probe}},\, W_t^{\mathrm{evid}}\}$ on $A_t$ gives $q_t^\pi \le \bar\rho \exp(-\mathcal I)$; the minimum with the raw UCB is again a UCB.
6. *Proposition 2.9.* With frozen $M^\star$, the active-success hazard admits the CP LCB; the snapshot-on/off gap is a diagnostic, not a certificate component.
7. *Corollary 2.10.* No-false survival lower bound, still-active upper bound, and disjoint decomposition $\{\tau_F > T\} = \{\tau_S \le T,\, \tau_S < \tau_F\} \dot\cup A_{T+1}$.
8. *Theorem 2.6.* All UCB/LCB quantities are simultaneously valid over the finite manifest; the argmax inherits the bound.
